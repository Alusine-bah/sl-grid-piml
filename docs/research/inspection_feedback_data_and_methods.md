# Build the inspection log before the model

No public dataset anywhere pairs real 11 kV feeder topology and head-meter energy with real field-inspection labels, so sl-grid-piml cannot be fully verified on borrowed data: the inspection-logging module EDSA crews would fill in is itself the most valuable verification dataset the project can obtain. Public data still covers the pieces separately. The State Grid of China (SGCC) set offers **42,372 customers with utility-supplied theft labels but no topology**, Indian discom energy-audit reports offer **800 real 11 kV feeders with input-versus-billed losses from 0% to 80% but no labels**, and UK DNO portals offer clean LV-feeder aggregates for injection tests. Real campaigns set the priors: **routine sweeps find irregularities in roughly 15–25% of visits, data-targeted campaigns in roughly 28–50%, and saturation sweeps of known hotspots in 54–80%**, with a few thousand kWh recovered per positive case. EDSA itself loses about half of what it buys (684 GWh purchased vs 344.5 GWh billed in 2023), has a nascent Revenue Protection Unit, publishes no inspection outcomes and no feeder data, and is heading toward a private operator with a sub-20% loss target. The right learning design for so few labels is not supervised ML or PU learning but a small Bayesian layer on top of the physics model, with logged selection probabilities, a 15% random-audit stream, Beta-Binomial area updates, a two-parameter calibration, and Wilson intervals on every reported hit rate. The module must never present model-arm hit rates as accuracy, never count "no access" as clean, and never label a customer a thief.

## EDSA loses half its energy and publishes none of the data needed to locate it

The headline numbers are consistent across sources even though their definitions differ. The Mission 300 National Energy Compact records **about 684 GWh purchased against 344.5 GWh billed in 2023**, commercial and technical losses above 50% in 2024 (53.2% in its annex), a postpaid collection ratio of 0.57, about 350,000 customers, 345,858 metered customers in 2025, and roughly 1,400 large customers producing over 40% of revenue ([Energy Compact](https://thedocs.worldbank.org/en/doc/ce215ab7fd4bf5cd190c8ddb32477fa6-0010012025/original/Sierra-Leone-National-Energy-Compact-Mission-300.pdf)). The Ministry of Finance puts 2023 total losses including non-payment at **60.9%**, splits them into roughly 40% "technical" and 14% commercial, reports overall collection at 86% after the prepaid shift, and describes Freetown's 514 km of 33/11 kV lines, about 1,000 km of "dilapidated" LV, and a distribution peak of about 80 MW against 105 MW unsuppressed demand ([MoF, Feb 2025](https://mof.gov.sl/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/Addressing-Systemic-Electricity-Distribution-and-Supply-Issues-in-Sierra-Leone.pdf)). Deloitte puts 2024 ATC&C above 51% ([Deloitte](https://www.deloitte.com/gh/en/services/consulting/perspectives/enhancing-edsa-energy-accountability-and-revenue-protection-for-sustainable-power-sector.html)). The 40/14 technical/commercial split is almost certainly a residual estimate, because nothing in the public record shows feeder or transformer energy metering; that unmeasured split is precisely the gap a physics-informed locator fills.

Two features of this environment shape the module directly. First, at ~50% loss theft is not a rare anomaly, so ranking feeders and areas by estimated loss beats binary "anomaly" flags, and untargeted hit rates in Freetown will likely exceed the 15–25% band seen at lower-loss utilities (an inference; no Sierra Leone hit-rate figure exists). Second, the average billed energy per customer is only about **985 kWh per year** (344.5 GWh ÷ ~350,000 customers, computed), and that average is inflated by the 1,400 large customers. International recovered-energy figures of 4,000–12,000 kWh per positive case therefore cannot be transplanted to residential Freetown; they plausibly fit only EDSA's large commercial and industrial accounts.

The institutional pieces are moving but none produces open data. The FCDO-funded ASI/Deloitte revenue-protection pilot in **Freetown Districts 80, 79 and 81** integrated customer, metering and network data, built partial GIS customer-to-transformer links, inspected large commercial users (finding faulty meters, illegal connections and billing irregularities), and advised on the structure and SOPs of EDSA's **Revenue Protection Unit**, but published only qualitative outcomes ([MyJoyOnline/ASI, Jun 2026](https://www.myjoyonline.com/asi-impact-series-protecting-revenue-powering-progress-in-sierra-leone/)). The **US$480M MCC compact** funds a main and back-up EDSA distribution dispatch centre with "centralized visibility" plus a US$123.6M distribution-and-access component ([MCC](https://www.mcc.gov/where-we-work/program/sierra-leone-compact/)). The IMF programme lists "inspections, prosecutions, public hotlines, staff accountability", 3,000 postpaid industrial meters and prepaid smart meters for government agencies ([Awoko, Feb 2026](https://awokonewspapersl.com/?p=153486)). The Compact promises audit teams, loss-reduction teams, customer-base mapping, AMI starting with large customers, and SOPs for connection, inspection and disconnection ([Energy Compact](https://thedocs.worldbank.org/en/doc/ce215ab7fd4bf5cd190c8ddb32477fa6-0010012025/original/Sierra-Leone-National-Energy-Compact-Mission-300.pdf)). Government has said operations will pass to a private operator by end-2026 without sale of EDSA ([Sierraloaded, Apr 2026](https://sierraloaded.sl/news/edsa-private-sector-management-end-2026/)), and unconfirmed press reports name Uganda's Umeme as sole bidder for a 15-year concession targeting **losses below 20%**, possibly starting in 2027 ([UgandaOnline, Aug 2026](https://www.ugandaonline.net/news/2026-08-31--umeme-poised-for-15-year-power-distribution-deal-in-sierra-leone--4d7de2100005/)). On data systems, only the vending side is visible: Holley supplied nearly 80,000 STS prepaid meters from 2019, with integration options for the existing CONLOG vending system ([Holley](https://www.holleymetering.com/sierra-leone)); the CIS/billing vendor is unnamed. Enforcement is criminal, with a specialised electricity court announced by the Chief Justice ([Premier Media](https://www.premiermedia-sl.com/?p=22656); [Judiciary](https://www.judiciary.gov.sl/?p=1718)), which means every inspection record may become evidence.

The practical consequence is that EDSA's realistic inputs are prepaid vending transactions, postpaid billing for large customers, partial transformer mapping in three districts, and manual or future-SCADA feeder readings. The tool should ingest CSV exports, treat reconciliation of messy records as core value, and target both EDSA's RPU and the incoming operator, whose concession KPIs will create demand for feeder-level loss baselines.

## Public data verifies the pieces, never the whole pipeline

**Customer-level labels.** The SGCC dataset is the only widely downloadable corpus with utility-supplied theft labels: 42,372 customers, daily kWh, 1 January 2014 to 31 October 2016, distributed as split zips with no stated licence and no description of how labels were obtained ([GitHub](https://github.com/henryRDlab/ElectricityTheftDetection); [Kaggle mirror](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/bensalem14/sgcc-dataset)). It remains the standard benchmark in 2025 papers ([Energy Reports 2025](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2352484725005360)). The frequently quoted figure of about 3,615 labelled thieves could not be verified from a primary source. SGCC can test billing-anomaly scoring but not localisation, because it has no feeders, head meters or transformer assignments. Grouping SGCC customers under virtual transformers would keep labels real while making the physics synthetic, and any write-up must say so. The large inspection-labelled corpora are all proprietary: the Brazilian CHOICE data with about 400K inspection records ([Glauner et al. survey](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1606.00626)), Endesa's ([Endesa](https://www.endesa.com/en/press/press-room/news/customers/endesa-detected-approximately-65000-cases-of-electricity-fraud-in-2018)), and JPS's. Other "theft datasets" are real load with synthetic attacks, such as the Mendeley OEDI-based set with six injected theft types ([Mendeley](https://data.mendeley.com/datasets/c3c7329tjj)) and the Irish CER trial, which requires an ISSDA application restricted to EEA or adequacy-decision countries, so a Bangladesh- or Sierra Leone-based requester likely cannot obtain it ([ISSDA](https://www.ucd.ie/issda/data/commissionforenergyregulationcer/)). A 2026 survey confirms that confirmed theft cases are scarce and confidential, hampering reproduction ([MDPI BDCC 2026](https://www.mdpi.com/2504-2289/10/9/297)).

**Feeder-level energy balance.** The closest public analogue to EDSA's head-meter-versus-billing balance is India's BEE-mandated periodic energy audit. TP Central Odisha's Q2 FY24-25 report lists feeder-wise received, consumed and billed energy for **800 of 1,286 metered 11 kV feeders**, with losses from 0% to 80%; only 6,658 of 90,587 distribution transformers are metered, and the LT/11 kV/33 kV losses are 26.49%/4.11%/2.95% ([TPCODL Q2 FY24-25](https://www.tpcentralodisha.com/pdf/energyaudit/Periodic_EA/EA_Report_Q2_FY24-25.pdf)). Multiple quarters exist ([Q4 FY23-24](https://www.tpcentralodisha.com/pdf/energyaudit/Periodic_EA/EA_Report_Q4_FY23-24.pdf), [Q1 FY24-25](https://www.tpcentralodisha.com/pdf/energyaudit/Periodic_EA/EA_Report_Q1%20FY24-25.pdf)), so the feeder-loss estimation and ranking stage can be tested against a real loss distribution and its quarter-to-quarter stability. The sparse DT metering mirrors EDSA's situation. Karnataka's experience warns what such data looks like in practice: only 55% DT metering in CESC by March 2018, about 14% of DT meters not recording, consumer-to-DT mapping that did not match the field, and cases where billed energy exceeded feeder input ([CSTEP 2019](https://cstep.in/drupal/sites/default/files/2019-02/CSTEP_WS_Energy_Auditing_Case_Study_Jan2019.pdf)). The tool should flag "billed > input" as a data-quality fault, not negative loss.

UK DNOs publish half-hourly smart-meter consumption aggregated to LV feeders or secondary substations, such as UK Power Networks (sites with more than five smart meters, registration required) ([UKPN](https://ukpowernetworks.opendatasoft.com/explore/dataset/ukpn-smart-meter-consumption-lv-feeder)) and SSEN ([SSEN](https://data-api.ssen.co.uk/dataset/ssen_smart_meter_prod_lv_feeder/resource/1cce1fb4-d7f4-4309-b9e3-943bd4d18618)). With low UK theft these work as clean control feeders for synthetic injection and false-alarm-rate testing. Regional priors come from aggregates: NERC's 2026 Q1 report gives disco-level ATC&C from 20.24% (Eko) to 69.66% (Kaduna) against a 16.92% target, with no feeder data ([NERC 2026 Q1](https://nerc.gov.ng/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/2026_Q1-Report.pdf)), and ESMAP's UPBEAT covers 80+ Sub-Saharan utilities for 2012–2020 ([UPBEAT](https://utilityperformance.energydata.info/about/)). For vending ingestion, the South African prepaid-metering replication data on openICPSR is the only candidate found, unlabelled and with unverified access terms ([openICPSR 113412](https://www.openicpsr.org/openicpsr/project/113412)).

| Verification target | Best public source | Real labels? | What it cannot test |
|---|---|---|---|
| Customer anomaly scoring | SGCC (42,372 customers) | Yes, provenance undocumented | Topology, localisation |
| Feeder loss estimation/ranking | TPCODL BEE energy audits (800 feeders) | No | Within-feeder location |
| Energy-balance false alarms | UKPN/SSEN LV-feeder aggregates | No (low-theft control) | Theft behaviour |
| Vending-record ingestion | openICPSR South Africa prepaid | No | Theft signal |
| Country/utility priors | NERC quarterlies, UPBEAT | No | Anything below utility level |
| End-to-end localisation + labels | None public | — | — |

The only route to real African inspection labels is a data-sharing agreement with a utility (EDSA's RPU, the FCDO pilot districts, ECG, Kenya Power or a Nigerian disco) or the records the module itself will generate.

## Field campaigns set the priors: 15–25% untargeted, 28–50% targeted

Documented hit rates fall into three bands. Broad sweeps: JPS audited 17,475 meters in early 2018 and found 2,798 irregularities (**16.0%**, computed), while also removing 69,894 illegal line taps ([Gleaner 2018](https://past.jamaica-gleaner.com/article/news/20180601/jps-unearths-almost-3000-irregularities-during-customer-meter-audits)); MP West Discom checked about 136,000 connections and found 33,500 irregularities (**24.6%**) ([FPJ 2025](https://www.freepressjournal.in/indore/indore-25discom-consumers-doing-irregularities)); Endesa's 2024 total of 71,000 fraud cases from about 428,000 verification inspections is **16.6%** (computed), and citizen tip-offs yielded about 20% ([Endesa 2025](https://www.endesa.com/en/press/press-room/news/energy-sector/electric-fraud-cases-2024)). Targeted campaigns: Endesa reported finding theft in about **one in two** inspections by 2020, double its 2017 rate ([Endesa 2021](https://www.endesa.com/en/press/press-room/news/customers/artificial-intelligence-inspections-against-electrical-fraud)); Naturgy's ML system reached up to **36%** on contracted customers and above 50% on uncontracted ones, against 21% for a competitor system ([Coma-Puig et al.](https://core.ac.uk/download/288626479.pdf)); a Rio de Janeiro AI tool reported **28%** with an average of 4,289 kWh recovered per positive ([Cenário Energia 2026](https://cenarioenergia.com.br/2026/07/06/inteligencia-artificial-eleva-eficiencia-de-distribuidoras-no-combate-a-furtos/)). Hotspot saturation: Endesa neighbourhood sweeps found fraud in **80%** of 1,232 supplies in Granada and **54%** of 983 in Seville ([Granada](https://www.endesa.com/en/press/press-room/news/energy-transition/smart-grids/fight-against-massive-fraud-grenada); [Seville](https://www.endesa.com/en/press/press-room/news/energy-transition/smart-grids/fight-against-massive-fraud-seville)). The nearest West African figure, ECG Ashanti West's 2,248 surcharges from 8,182 visits (**27.5%**, computed) with 3.02 GWh recovered, is itself a targeted figure ([MyJoyOnline 2026](https://www.myjoyonline.com/a-r-ecg-surcharges-over-2200-customers-for-illegal-connections-recovers-over-gh%C2%A24-3m-in-2025/)).

The Endesa conflict (50% claimed versus 16.6% computed) teaches the main lesson: published hit rates rarely define their denominator, so a "targeted" rate is meaningless without the random baseline beside it. Doubling the base rate is a realistic upper target for lift, while naive expert rules can perform at chance (Brazilian Boolean systems reached AUC about 0.47) ([Glauner et al. survey](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1606.00626)).

Recovered energy per positive is heavy-tailed: about 4,289 kWh in Rio, about 8,800 kWh per apprehended customer in ECG Accra West (3.3 GWh from 374 cases) ([GNA 2025](https://gna.org.gh/2025/03/accra-west-ecg-recovers-over-gh₵-7-million-from-illegal-activities)), and about 12,200 kWh per Endesa case, skewed by cannabis farms (computed). One Naturgy case exceeded 260,000 kWh against typical annual use of 3,500 kWh ([Coma-Puig & Carmona](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2009.13437v1)). Back-billing is typically capped at about one year in Spain and Madhya Pradesh ([Naturgy paper](https://core.ac.uk/download/288626479.pdf); [FPJ 2025](https://www.freepressjournal.in/indore/indore-25discom-consumers-doing-irregularities)), which bounds recoverable energy per case. No source published cost per inspection, crew productivity, inspector false-negative rate, no-access share or recidivism rate, so these must be user parameters. The rapid re-appearance of line taps at JPS argues for scheduled revisits.

Outcomes are multi-class everywhere. Naturgy separates Fraud, Incident (non-fraud meter fault, still NTL), Correct, Unknown (for example refused access) and No result, and leaves Unknown unlabelled rather than negative ([Naturgy paper](https://core.ac.uk/download/288626479.pdf)). Brazil records each irregularity on a formal TOI with recovered kWh ([Cenário Energia](https://cenarioenergia.com.br/2026/07/06/inteligencia-artificial-eleva-eficiencia-de-distribuidoras-no-combate-a-furtos/)), and ECG records apprehension, disconnection, surcharge and prosecution ([GNA 2025](https://gna.org.gh/2025/03/accra-west-ecg-recovers-over-gh₵-7-million-from-illegal-activities)). The biases are well documented. In Brazil, 620K of 3.6M customers had been inspected, and an inspected-versus-population classifier reached a Matthews correlation of 0.224 on location alone and 0.273 on all features, above the 0.2 threshold for meaningful covariate shift ([Glauner et al. 2017](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1702.03767)). Naturgy saw over-represented Madrid inflate scores, regional technician performance vary, and recidivists over-controlled ([Coma-Puig & Carmona](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2009.13437v1)). Brazilian labels are noisy because of bribery, threats and sophisticated fraud ([Glauner et al. survey](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1606.00626)). Crew safety is a real constraint: Johannesburg's City Power suspended services after audit crews were held hostage and threatened with firearms ([The Citizen 2025](https://www.citizen.co.za/news/south-africa/city-power-rising-attacks-staff-suspends-services)).

## Few labels call for Bayesian layers on physics, not machine learning

The physics model's structural advantage is that its core estimate needs no labels and therefore inherits no inspection-selection bias. Inspection feedback should calibrate and correct it, not replace it with a classifier trained on biased labels. The literature supports this ordering. Greedy top-k selection, which investigates the highest predicted probabilities and retrains on the results, can be inconsistent, while randomised selection appeared consistent in simulation, and maximum-likelihood updating stays valid when selection probabilities are accounted for ([Revelas, Boldea & Werker 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.18739v1)). In IRS audit selection, greedy policies produced about 16% bias in population estimates, even a small random component greatly reduced it, and risk-tilted random sampling within bins matched greedy reward while giving unbiased Horvitz-Thompson estimates. Thompson sampling left about 75% of units with zero selection probability, breaking those estimators ([Henderson et al., AAAI 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.11910)). Post-hoc reweighting without known propensities was inconclusive at Naturgy even with about 310,000 labels ([Coma-Puig & Carmona](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2009.13437v1)).

PU learning is the right theory for interpreting labels but the wrong tool to deploy early. Inspection labels are positive-unlabelled: confirmed theft is positive, "clean" is a noisy negative, and uninspected units are unlabelled. Elkan and Noto's rescaling requires labelled positives to be a random sample of all positives ([Elkan & Noto 2008](https://members.cbio.mines-paristech.fr/~jvert/svn/bibli/local/Elkan2008Learning.pdf)), which targeting violates, and the SAR relaxation requires a known labelling propensity e(x) ([Bekker et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1808.08755)). That is exactly what logged selection probabilities provide. NTL PU papers reporting metrics "approaching 99%" without baselines deserve little weight ([Springer 2024](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s13198-024-02333-8)). Supervised, PU and importance-weighted models should wait until the log holds thousands of outcomes. Until then, one-class anomaly scores on prepaid vending (purchase gaps, sudden drops) can rank meters within an area flagged by the physics model. This two-stage pattern, network method to localise and data method to rank within, matches the literature, including Raggi et al.'s state-estimation-style bad-data analysis on a real 1,682-bus system ([Raggi et al. 2020](https://bv.fapesp.br/en/publicacao/188445/non-technical-loss-identification-by-using-data-analytics-an)) and Coma-Puig's shift to ranking by energy to recover ([Machine Learning 2022](https://link.springer.com/10.1007/s10994-021-06051-1)).

Sample sizes make the case for humility concrete. With a 25% hit rate, 95% Wilson intervals span 8.9–53.2% at n = 12, 13.8–41.1% at n = 36 and 17.5–34.3% at n = 100 (computed). Wald intervals have erratic coverage at these sizes, so use Wilson, Agresti-Coull or Jeffreys ([Brown, Cai & DasGupta 2001](https://projecteuclid.org/euclid.ss/1009213286)). Showing at 80% power and one-sided α = 0.05 that the model beats a known 10% base rate needs about **69** model-picked inspections if the true rate is 20%, or about **20** if it is 30%. A two-arm model-versus-random comparison needs about **157 per arm** for 10% versus 20%, **49** for 10% versus 30%, and **64** for 20% versus 40% (computed from standard formulas). At EDSA's base rates the realistic first milestone is "the model arm hit rate exceeds the random arm with non-overlapping intervals" after one to two hundred inspections, not a precision figure after twenty.

## Recommended design for the inspection-logging module

**Selection and logging.** Each inspection round, the tool generates a work list from a mixed policy and records, at the moment of selection, the probability π_i that each area or meter was put on the list. The default policy reserves **15% of the round's budget for a stratified random audit** (stratified by feeder and by the physics model's risk tercile), with an allowed range of 10–20%. It spends the remaining 85% on risk-tilted sampling: areas are ranked by expected recoverable value, P(theft) × expected kWh × tariff × collectability − inspection cost, and sampled without replacement with probabilities proportional to that score, subject to a floor of **π_min = 0.02** per area per round so no area ever has zero probability. Pure top-k is not offered as a default. The 15% figure reconciles the 10–20% recommendation from the bandit literature with the 5–10% practitioner suggestion. With a 30-inspection monthly budget, 15% yields about 54 random inspections a year, enough for a base-rate interval of roughly ±12 percentage points, while costing little recovery. Thompson sampling over area Beta posteriors is allowed only with the same floor and with π estimated by Monte-Carlo replay and logged.

**Data fields.** Every record must stand up as court evidence and as a statistical observation.

| Group | Fields |
|---|---|
| Identity | inspection_id, round_id, feeder_id, DT_id (nullable), area_id, account/meter_no (nullable), GPS, address-match flag |
| Selection | selection_arm (random / model / tip-off / routine / follow-up), π_i, model score and rank at selection, model version, physics estimate x̂ ± σ for the area |
| Execution | date and time, crew IDs, escort/safety flag, premises in area, premises actually checked (coverage f), time on site |
| Outcome | outcome category (below), evidence type (photo, seized device, meter test), photo hashes, meter reading and seal numbers |
| Value | estimated kWh recovered (method stated), back-bill period (≤ cap), back-bill amount, fine, amount actually collected (later), follow-up action and revisit date |
| Audit | supervisor sign-off, dispute/appeal status, label-revision history |

**Outcome categories.** Use multi-class codes, not a theft/no-theft flag: THEFT_BYPASS, METER_TAMPER, ILLEGAL_CONNECTION (no account or line tap), METER_FAULT_NONFRAUD (still NTL, still recoverable), BILLING_ERROR (data or mapping fault), CABLE_OR_NETWORK_FAULT (the tool also locates faults), CLEAN, NO_ACCESS_REFUSED, NOT_FOUND_ADDRESS_ERROR and ABORTED_SAFETY. For learning, the first five count as positive NTL findings and CLEAN as a noisy negative. NO_ACCESS, NOT_FOUND and ABORTED are missing, never negative, and are excluded from hit-rate denominators but reported as counts. Refusal of access may itself correlate with theft, so its rate per area should be tracked separately.

**Update maths.** The physics core remains the bounded WLS min (z − h(x))ᵀR⁻¹(z − h(x)) + (x − x₀)ᵀP₀⁻¹(x − x₀) with 0 ≤ x_j ≤ x_max,j. Inspection outcomes enter in three ways. First, recovered energy enters as a **one-sided pseudo-measurement**: x_j ≥ E_rec,j / (B_j + E_rec,j) × (1 − slack), never an equality, because partial coverage makes recovered energy a lower bound. Second, a clean result shrinks the prior without zeroing it, using P(hotspot | clean) = p(1 − s_d f) / [p(1 − s_d f) + (1 − p)], where f is the coverage fraction and s_d the crew detection sensitivity. Third, each area carries a **Beta-Binomial hotspot probability** θ_j ~ Beta(α₀, β₀), updated to Beta(α₀ + k_j, β₀ + n_j − k_j) after k_j positives in n_j valid inspections. Hierarchical pooling (logit θ_j = μ_feeder + u_j, u_j ~ N(0, τ²), or empirical-Bayes Beta-Binomial across areas) lets inspected areas inform uninspected ones. Once 20–50 valid outcomes exist, fit a **two-parameter logistic calibration** y ~ Bernoulli(σ(a + b·x̂_j/σ_j)), weighted by 1/π_i, so that the probabilities the tool displays are honest. These design formulas are standard state-estimation and Bayesian practice. No paper was found that feeds NTL inspection outcomes into a WLS model as pseudo-measurements, so this is synthesis, not a cited result.

**Default priors (all overridable, all labelled as defaults).**

| Parameter | Default | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Random-arm hit rate (high-loss utility) | Beta(1, 3), mean 25%, equivalent sample size 4 | Sweeps 16–25% at lower-loss utilities; EDSA ~50% loss suggests higher |
| Model-arm hit rate before evidence | Beta(1.4, 2.6), mean 35% | Targeted 28–50%; doubling the base is the upper benchmark |
| Area θ_j seed | mean = P(x_j > t \| WLS), equivalent sample size 2–5 | Keeps physics dominant until labels accumulate |
| Crew sensitivity s_d | 0.8 (assumption) | No published inspector false-negative rate |
| kWh per positive, large customers | median ~4,000–9,000, heavy-tailed | Rio 4,289; ECG Accra West ~8,800 |
| kWh per positive, residential | user-set; must not exceed local consumption × back-bill period | EDSA averages ~985 kWh/customer/year billed |
| Back-bill cap | 12 months | Spain, Madhya Pradesh practice |
| Cost per inspection, inspections/crew/day | user-set, no default | No source found |

**Evaluation.** Report each arm's hit rate with Wilson 95% intervals, with the random arm as the base rate. Report lift as the ratio of arms with a bootstrap interval, kWh recovered per inspection with a bootstrap interval (heavy tails), NDCG or energy recovered in the top k rather than precision@k alone, the Horvitz-Thompson population theft rate p̂ = (1/N) Σ y_i / π_i, and the Brier score (reliability bins only after about 100 outcomes). No-access counts appear beside every rate. A quarterly **coverage-bias check** trains an inspected-versus-all-areas classifier on area features and reports its Matthews correlation, with values above about 0.2 meaning inspections are drifting into a narrow subset. The module should display "insufficient evidence" until the sample-size thresholds above are met.

**What the module must not claim.** It must not present model-arm hit rates as accuracy or recall, since recall is unmeasurable from inspection data. It must not treat CLEAN as proof of no theft, or count NO_ACCESS as clean. It must not name or label any customer a thief: outputs are ranked locations for field verification, and only the field record with its evidence carries a finding. It must not claim superiority over random targeting before roughly 50–150 inspections per arm, or validation on "real data" when the feeder physics was synthetic (as with re-grouped SGCC). It must not present the technical/non-technical split as measured when it is estimated. And it must not transplant foreign kWh-per-case figures to Freetown households.

## Conclusion

Verifying sl-grid-piml is less a search for the right dataset than an exercise in experimental design. Every public resource validates one stage and none validates the chain, while the one end-to-end dataset that matters, with real feeders, real physics estimates and real inspection outcomes with known selection probabilities, does not yet exist anywhere and could be created by this module in Freetown. That reframes the project's pitch to EDSA, its Revenue Protection Unit, the FCDO pilot team and the incoming operator. The offer is less "a theft detector" than "the first honest, auditable measurement of where losses are and how well targeting works", which is what a sub-20% loss concession KPI will require.

The deeper design insight is that the 15% random audit is not a cost but the instrument that makes every other number credible. Without it, Endesa's own published figures cannot be reconciled, Brazilian models learned the inspectors' map rather than the thieves', and greedy loops can converge on the wrong answer. A tool that logs π, keeps its physics core label-free, and lets a handful of Beta parameters absorb field evidence will be slower to claim success but far harder to discredit in a specialised electricity court or a concession audit.
